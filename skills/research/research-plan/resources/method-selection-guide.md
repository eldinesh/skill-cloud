# Method selection guide

Pick the method from the decision, not from habit. Every row assumes a clinical-enterprise engagement unless noted.

## Study types

| Type | When | Typical methods | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generative | You don't yet know what to build; the problem needs defining | 1:1 interviews, contextual inquiry, diary study | Unmet needs, JTBD signals, opportunity areas |
| Descriptive | Problem known; context and workflow need full understanding | Contextual inquiry, shadowing, journey mapping inputs, card sort | Current-state workflow, mental models |
| Evaluative | A design or prototype exists and needs testing | Usability testing, heuristic evaluation, first-click/tree testing | Severity-ranked issues, task success rates |
| Causal | Something is live; you need to know what's actually happening and why | Analytics review, funnel analysis, A/B testing, targeted survey | Behavioral patterns, quantified validation |

## Decision table

| You need to… | Use… | Type | Realistic clinical timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover unmet needs in a workflow | Generative interviews (5–8 per role) | Generative | 3–5 weeks incl. recruiting |
| See the work as it actually happens | Contextual inquiry / shadowing | Descriptive | 2–4 weeks + site access lead time |
| Capture behavior over days/weeks | Diary study | Generative | 4–6 weeks |
| Test a design concept or prototype | Usability test, 3–5 per round | Evaluative | 1–2 weeks per round |
| Check navigation/terminology models | Card sort (15–20) or tree test | Descriptive | 2 weeks |
| Validate at scale with numbers | Survey (100+) anchored on prior qual | Causal | 2–3 weeks |
| Get a fast expert read before testing | Heuristic evaluation | Evaluative | 2–3 days |

## Sample-size logic (cite this in the plan)

- Interviews: 5–8 **per distinct role/segment** — saturation, not statistics. Segment by role, not site.
- Usability: 3–5 per round catches ~85% of issues; rounds beat headcount (3×3 > 1×9).
- Card sorts: 15–20 for stable agreement patterns. Surveys: 100+ per segment you'll cut by.
- Mixed roles in one clinical workflow = separate segments. A nurse and a pharmacist do not average.

## The runner-up paragraph

Stakeholders challenge methods ("why not just a survey?"). The plan must contain the rejected alternative and the mechanism it fails on:

- *Survey rejected:* surveys measure stated attitudes at scale; RQ1–RQ3 ask about unarticulated workflow behavior, which self-report distorts.
- *Interviews rejected:* interviews capture recalled behavior; the decision needs observed task success on a concrete design → usability test.
- *Usability test rejected:* tests evaluate a solution; no solution exists yet — the risk is building the wrong thing → generative interviews.
- *Analytics rejected:* analytics show what, not why; the decision hinges on why users abandon → pair a funnel read with interviews.

## Clinical-setting adjustments

- Session length: plan the 25–30 minute version first; the 60-minute version is the luxury case.
- Contextual inquiry needs badge/escort approval, infection-control rules, and a ruling on patient-adjacent observation — name these as plan dependencies with owners.
- Remote sessions: assume VDI, locked-down browsers, and no installs. Pilot the tech with the champion before fieldwork.
- Recruiting runs 2–3× consumer timelines. Overbook 20–30%; clinical no-shows are operational, not rudeness.
