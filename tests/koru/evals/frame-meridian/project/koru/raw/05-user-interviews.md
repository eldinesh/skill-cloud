# User interview transcripts

Four interviews run by Meridian's operations team last month, before Koru was involved. Each was about 30 minutes. Auto-transcribed and lightly cleaned. Interviewer is "Int".

## Interview 1: P01, charge nurse, medical-surgical unit, Meridian North (days, 11 years)

**Int:** Talk me through the last time you needed something you didn't have.

**P01:** Yesterday. We ran low on wound dressings, the silicone foam ones. I checked the supply room, two left. So I went into UnitDesk, which takes forever to load on the workstation, found the form, and then you have to pick from this dropdown of a thousand things with names like "DRSG FOAM SIL 10X10". I put the request in. Then I called materials anyway, because if you don't call, nothing happens.

**Int:** Why call if it's in the system?

**P01:** Because I've got no idea if anyone saw it. It just says "Submitted". It said "Submitted" for a request I made in August. I don't know if that means it's coming or it's dead.

**Int:** How much time does this take you in a shift?

**P01:** Depends. Some days nothing. Some days an hour. It's not the form, it's the chasing. Calling, waiting on hold, walking down to stores.

**Int:** What do you do so you don't run out?

**P01:** *[laughs]* Honestly? We keep a stash. Everybody does. When stuff comes in, we take a bit extra and keep it in a cupboard. Then of course the next unit runs out.

**Int:** If the system suggested what to order, would that help?

**P01:** Suggest, maybe, if it's right. But I want to see why. If it says order 40 and we use 10 a week, I'm not trusting it. And I don't want it ordering on its own. I know my patients. It doesn't.

**Int:** What about a chat assistant you could ask?

**P01:** I don't have time to chat with a computer. I want to see where my thing is. Just tell me where it is and when it's coming.

**Int:** Anything else?

**P01:** The discharge stuff is a nightmare. If a patient needs a walker or oxygen at home, I don't even know who to send that to. Sometimes UnitDesk, sometimes I email the plan, sometimes the social worker does it. Nobody knows.

## Interview 2: P02, travel nurse, nights, Meridian North (on contract, 7 weeks in)

**Int:** How do you raise a request?

**P02:** I don't, really. I don't have a UnitDesk login. There's a shared one taped inside the supply room door, but it logs out every ten minutes. Usually I ask the charge nurse, or I just call stores. At night stores is one person, so you call and call.

**Int:** What happens at night when something runs out?

**P02:** You borrow from another unit. You walk to the ED and beg. Last week we ran out of IV start kits at 3am. I walked two floors.

**Int:** Did anyone log that?

**P02:** No. Who would I tell? It's not my hospital. I'm here for 13 weeks.

**Int:** Do you use your phone for work?

**P02:** Everybody does. We text the charge nurse. We're not supposed to.

**Int:** If you could change one thing?

**P02:** Let me ask for stuff myself, and tell me if anyone's actually bringing it. Every hospital I go to has a different system. This one's one of the worse ones, no offence.

## Interview 3: P03, materials manager, Meridian Central (days, 6 years)

**Int:** What does a normal day look like?

**P03:** I start by going through overnight requests in UnitDesk, about 60 to 80. Maybe a third are duplicates, the same unit asking for the same thing twice because they didn't hear back. Then there are the phone calls, which aren't in the system at all. Then I check the ERP to see what we have, which is last night's count, so I walk to the stockroom to check anyway.

**Int:** Where do things go wrong?

**P03:** Item names. Nurses type "gloves medium" and there are four kinds of medium gloves. I call them back to ask which. And over-ordering. Units ask for 50 when they need 10, because they're scared of running out. Then stuff expires in their cupboards.

**Int:** Would AI suggestions help you?

**P03:** If it could tell me "this unit normally uses 12 a week, they've asked for 50", that would be gold. Flag it, let me decide. If it ordered on its own I'd be cleaning up its mess. We tried a vendor auto-reorder in 2022. I spent three months returning stock.

**Int:** What about approvals over $500?

**P03:** Most of those are equipment, not supplies. Supplies are cheap per line. The approval isn't the slow bit. The slow bit is me working out what they actually want, and then the delivery round, which is twice a day.

**Int:** Twice a day?

**P03:** 10am and 4pm. If your request is approved at 4:05, you get it tomorrow morning. Nobody on the units knows that.

**Int:** What would you want from a new system?

**P03:** One list, no duplicates, real item names, and units can see when their stuff is on the next round. Honestly, if they could see that, half the phone calls would stop.

## Interview 4: P04, care navigator, Meridian Plan (3 years)

**Int:** How do discharge requests reach you?

**P04:** Every way possible. Email from a nurse, a UnitDesk request that someone forwards to us, a phone call to our main line, a message from the hospital social worker. Sometimes the member calls us after they're already home without the equipment.

**Int:** What do you need to act on one?

**P04:** Member ID, what they need, when they're going home, and the address. Usually I get one of those. The nurse doesn't know the member ID because the hospital system shows the hospital record number, not the plan ID. So I spend time matching people.

**Int:** Can you see the clinical reason?

**P04:** No, and I shouldn't. But I need to know it's ordered by a clinician for coverage. That's a yes or no, not the notes.

**Int:** How often do things go wrong?

**P04:** Every week. Last month a member went home without oxygen because the email went to someone on leave. Those are the ones that end up as complaints, or readmissions.

**Int:** If there was one system?

**P04:** I'd want to see discharge requests for our members, with a date, and be able to say "got it, it's arranged for Tuesday" so the nurse knows. That's it. I don't need the gloves.
