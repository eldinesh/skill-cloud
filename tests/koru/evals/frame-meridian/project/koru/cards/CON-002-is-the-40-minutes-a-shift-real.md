# CON-002 · Is the 40 minutes a shift real?

## Positions

- **A. About 40 minutes a shift.** Marcus Lee, relaying what nurses tell him (FND-033, stakeholder); the earlier synthesis calls it "strong evidence, confirmed by the Director of Nursing" (DAT-007); the PRD repeats it as an estimate. All three trace back to one source.
- **B. Median 18 minutes, 90th percentile 52.** Q3 time-and-motion audit of 40 shifts (FND-026, observed), which excluded time spent on the phone away from the unit. Unit and role mix of the audited shifts not given.
- **C. None some days, an hour on others, and it is the chasing.** P01 (FND-003, story, one participant).

## Weighing

These may not conflict: 40 sits between the audit median and the 90th percentile, and the audit left out the phone time that P01 names as the main cost, and 10,970 calls to stores were logged in Q3 (FND-030). What is wrong is the use of 40 as a fact: it is relayed, circular in the synthesis, and not measured. The audit is the only measured figure, and it is partial.

Consequence for the Brief and for Steer: do not promise "40 minutes back". Set the baseline by a short diary or shadow study that includes phone and walking time, and report median and p90, since the problem is uneven (some days nothing, some an hour).

Status: **carried**, not resolved. It is resolved when a baseline is measured; until then the Brief names the measure, not the number. Position C is one charge nurse (1 of 4 interviewees, one unit type), and the audit's unit and role mix is not given, so neither is a baseline.
