# Research synthesis: Requests at Meridian

**Prepared by:** Meridian Operations Improvement team
**Based on:** 4 user interviews, 2 site visits (Meridian North and Central), UnitDesk data review
**Date:** October

## Summary

Requests at Meridian are slow, opaque and duplicated. Nurses lose around 40 minutes per shift to request admin. Staff want a faster, smarter tool, and there is strong appetite for AI, including a conversational assistant. Manager approvals for requests over $500 are the main cause of delay.

## Who we spoke to

| Persona | Role | Sites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Charge Nurse | Raises and chases supply requests for the unit | North |
| The Materials Manager | Receives, checks and fulfils requests | Central |
| The Care Navigator | Arranges members' needs after discharge | Meridian Plan |

## Key findings

### 1. Nobody knows where a request is

Once a request is submitted, UnitDesk shows "Submitted" until it's closed. Staff call materials to check, which creates more work for both sides. *Strong evidence: raised in every interview.*

### 2. Nurses lose around 40 minutes per shift to requests

Confirmed by the Director of Nursing. This includes finding the form, picking items and chasing. *Strong evidence.*

### 3. Approvals are the main bottleneck

Requests over $500 wait for a manager, and managers are busy on the floor. Removing or automating approvals would speed up fulfilment significantly. *Medium evidence.*

### 4. Item names cause errors

UnitDesk item names are free text or cryptic catalogue codes. Materials managers spend time clarifying what was meant. *Strong evidence.*

### 5. Units hoard supplies

Units keep extra stock "just in case", which causes shortages elsewhere and waste through expiry. *Strong evidence.*

### 6. Staff want an AI assistant

Nurses are open to AI and would like a chat assistant to raise and track requests. *Medium evidence.*

### 7. Discharge needs fall between the hospital and the plan

Discharge requests arrive by email, phone and UnitDesk, and members sometimes go home without equipment. *Strong evidence.*

## Journey: a supply request today

1. Nurse notices low stock
2. Finds a free workstation and opens UnitDesk
3. Searches for the item
4. Submits request
5. Request waits for approval (if over $500)
6. Materials manager reviews
7. Item is picked and delivered
8. Nurse calls to check status (often several times)

## Opportunities

- Real-time request status for staff
- AI chat assistant to raise and track requests
- Smart item search with plain names
- Remove manual approvals using AI
- Predict demand to prevent stockouts
- One intake for discharge needs
